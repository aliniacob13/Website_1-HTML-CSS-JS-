Golden Globes

La La Land continued its seemingly unstoppable charge toward the Oscars with a record-breaking night at the Golden Globes:

    Best Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy
    Best Director – Motion Picture
    Best Actor in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy
    Best Actress in a Motion Picture – Musical or Comedy
    Best Screenplay – Motion Picture
    Best Original Score – Motion Picture
    Best Original Song – Motion Picture

SAG Awards

Screen Actors Guild Awards (also known as SAG Awards) are accolades given by the Screen Actors Guild-American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (SAG-AFTRA). The award was founded in 1995 to recognize outstanding performances in movie and prime time television.
SAG awards focus both on individual performances and on the work of the entire ensemble of a drama series and comedy series, and the cast of a motion picture.
A lot of the members who vote for these awards are also members of the Academy, so whoevers gets an award at this ceremony is very likely to get a golden statue at the Oscars.
Emma Stone received the award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role, paving her way to grab an Oscar at the Dolby Theather in LA on February 26th.

Academy Awards

La La Land was nominated for 14 Academy Awards, tying the record for most nominations with Titanic and All About Eve. The film won 6 Oscars, including:

    Best Director for Damien Chazelle, who became the youngest person to win this award
    Best Actress for Emma Stone
    Best Original Score for Justin Hurwitz
    Best Original Song for "City of Stars"
    Best Achievement in Cinematography for Linus Sandgren
    Best Achievement in Production Design

During the Oscars ceremony, presenter Faye Dunaway incorrectly announced that La La Land had won Best Picture, reading from the card Warren Beatty opened, which was actually a duplicate of the Best Actress card for Emma Stone. After the cast and crew of La La Land took the stage, it took the show's producers more than two minutes (during which nearly three speeches were made) to fix the mistake. The actual winner was Moonlight.
